# Home Page Audio Summary Script

Use this text for the future feature: "If you are busy, listen to the summary."

## English Voice Script

Welcome to the College of Project Controls and Management.

This page gives you a quick overview of what the College offers to professionals and employers working in project management, project controls and PMO environments.

The central idea is simple: helping people lead projects with clarity, control and confidence through professional programmes, specialist modules and development pathways linked to real workplace responsibilities.

At the top of the page, the main message introduces the College as a specialist provider for professionals and organisations working in complex project environments. The learning is not only theoretical. It is designed around live responsibilities, guidance from experienced practitioners and clear progression routes from capability development to career growth.

From the hero section, users can explore programmes, compare programmes or learn more about funding and access options.

The page then shows trusted partner or organisation logos, giving users a quick signal of professional credibility and institutional relationships.

Next, the page introduces the professional programmes.

There are three main programmes.

The first is Project Controls Professional Level 6. This programme is for professionals who want to build advanced capability in planning, scheduling, cost, earned value, risk, governance and performance information for complex project environments.

The second is Associate Project Manager Level 4. This programme helps professionals build broader capability across project delivery, including governance, planning, scheduling, cost, risk and stakeholder management, while applying learning directly to real work.

The third is Certified PMO Professional Level 6. This programme focuses on advanced PMO capability, including strategic governance, integrated controls, risk, quality, stakeholder leadership and evidence-based reporting.

Below the programme cards, the page gives a simple comparison to help users understand which programme best fits their responsibilities. The comparison looks at programme level, discipline and the type of professional each route is best suited to.

The page then gives quick guidance for choosing the right programme. It starts with the user's current role, then the capability they need to strengthen, then whether they need a full programme or targeted specialist development, and finally the professional direction they want to build towards.

After the programme section, the page presents specialist modules.

These modules help users build one specific capability at a time. Examples include Project Management Professional, AI in Project Controls, Risk Management, Scheduling Professional, Earned Value Management, Project Planning and Controls, Managing Successful Programmes, Management of Portfolios and PMO.

The page then introduces the professional pathways.

There are three main pathways.

The Operational Pathway is for planners, schedulers, cost professionals and project controls practitioners who need reliable information to support day-to-day delivery.

The Strategic Pathway is for senior project controls, PMO, programme, portfolio and governance professionals who support complex decisions and need to connect project performance with organisational priorities.

The Chartered Pathway is for experienced professionals who want to build stronger professional evidence and prepare for an independent professional assessment route, such as APM Chartered Project Professional, where the relevant eligibility and assessment requirements are met.

If none of the standard pathways fits perfectly, the page explains that a tailored six-credit route can be discussed around current duties, employer needs and available workplace evidence.

The page then shows the project-driven sectors served by the College.

This section explains that project controls capability can be applied in complex environments such as construction and infrastructure, energy and utilities, engineering, manufacturing and aerospace, and public sector or council delivery. The aim is to apply planning, cost, risk, governance and delivery performance capability in sectors where reliable information and confident decisions matter.

Next, the page moves into funding, eligibility and support.

The funding and costs section explains that routes and courses have commercial values, and that some eligible programmes may be supported through apprenticeship funding arrangements where learner, employer and programme conditions are met. It also highlights additional professional support, including professional memberships, professional clubs, Institute of Project Controls membership, private healthcare during the programme, wellbeing support and optional assessments that help identify professional or learning needs.

The page also refers to possible support from the Institute of Project Controls Fund. Support may cover up to seventy-five percent of tuition fees for some eligible unemployed or self-employed learners, or up to fifty percent for some eligible employed learners, with the employer contributing the remaining share. All support is subject to review and approval.

After funding, the page presents the eligibility checker section.

This section helps users take a first step with confidence. They can use a quick eligibility check to receive an initial indication of whether an apprenticeship route may be suitable. The page makes clear that this is not a final decision, and that final eligibility depends on a full review of personal circumstances, employment, role fit and employer eligibility.

The page also lists things users should check before applying, such as UK residency, right to work, not being enrolled in another government-funded training programme at the same time, being in paid employment in England, and having an employer that is based in England and uses the Apprenticeship Service.

The page then introduces the Institute of Project Controls.

This section explains that the Institute is a professional accreditation and support body focused on advancing project controls capability across the wider project delivery community. It highlights trusted accreditation, professional growth and potential tuition fee support for eligible learners, with links to explore accreditation or learn more about the Institute.

Next, the page shows short courses.

This section is for users who want to build a specific specialist capability without immediately joining a full programme. Short courses focus on areas such as planning, cost, risk, AI, PMO and professional certification preparation. Users can browse courses through a carousel or move to the full short courses page.

The next section is for employers.

This section speaks to organisations that want to develop their teams. Employers can build a capability plan that targets skill gaps, develops existing talent, connects learning to live projects and systems, and selects either full programmes or targeted specialist modules based on role needs.

The page then presents case studies.

This section shows practical examples of project controls capability in action. The aim is to show how structured learning, workplace evidence and stronger controls can support better delivery decisions. Published case studies from the dashboard appear here for users to explore.

After case studies, the page introduces learning from practitioners.

This section presents mentors or experts who contribute to the programmes. The idea is that learning does not only come from training content. It also comes from people with professional experience in governance, project controls, PMO and programme management.

The page then shows coaching and support.

This section focuses on support for learners during their development journey. It presents available coaches or support specialists and the areas where they can help, such as understanding the role, organising evidence, preparing for reviews and turning learning into workplace practice.

Next, the page introduces shared learning and events.

This part points users towards events, masterclasses or shared learning sessions. The purpose is to give users additional opportunities to learn, meet the programme team and understand professional topics in a live setting.

After that, the page shows the journal section.

This section presents the latest articles and insights. The articles help users understand topics such as project controls, funding, pathway differences, governance, project management and professional development.

The page then presents professional experience.

This section displays approved reviews and experiences from professionals and employers. If no reviews are currently published, the page encourages people who have completed a programme to share their experience and explain how they applied their learning at work.

At the end of the page, there is a section on professional development and recognition.

This section explains that the learning and support provided by the College is separate from external professional awards. Memberships, examinations and chartered status remain subject to the eligibility rules and assessment processes of the relevant professional bodies.

In summary, the home page helps users understand four things.

First, what the College is and what it offers.

Second, which programmes, pathways and specialist modules are available.

Third, how learning can be connected to real work, either for an individual professional or for an employer.

And fourth, what the next step could be: exploring a programme, comparing pathways, checking eligibility, reading a case study or article, or speaking with the team.

If you are busy or cannot read the full page right now, this summary gives you the big picture. The College helps professionals and organisations build stronger capability in project management, project controls and PMO through professional programmes, specialist modules, potential funding support, expert guidance, useful knowledge content and practical workplace evidence.
